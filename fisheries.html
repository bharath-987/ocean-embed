How is the PFZ Index Calculated?

The Potential Fishing Zone (PFZ) Index combines 85% model-derived thermal dynamics with a 15% surface primary productivity proxy:

85% Model-Derived Physical Drivers
  • Thermocline Shoaling (35%): Shallower thermocline depth (<80m) compresses pelagic habitat toward the euphotic zone, concentrating forage fish.
  • Vertical Thermal Gradient / Upwelling (35%): Rate of temperature change (dT/dz) across 0–50m. Strong gradients indicate cold, nutrient-rich water ascending to the surface.
  • Horizontal Thermal Front Gradient (15%): Spatial SST gradient magnitude (||∇H SST|| in °C / 100 km). Thermal fronts represent convergent boundaries where plankton and biomass concentrate.
15% Estimated Heuristic
  • Surface Primary Productivity Proxy (15%): Evaluated from upwelling strength and cyclonic Sea Level Anomaly (SLA eddy pumping) as an ecological food-web baseline.
Physical Interpretation of Advisory Tiers:
Elevated (≥ 0.70) Co-occurrence of strong vertical thermal gradient (>1.5°C/50m), shallow thermocline (<80m), and active horizontal thermal front (>0.5°C/100km). Historically associated with pelagic fish aggregation. Auto-highlighted on the map overlay. Not validated against commercial catch data.
Moderate (0.40–0.69) Partially favorable conditions; intermediate thermocline or moderate thermal gradient. Mixed subsurface aggregation signal. Available in detail panel via direct coordinate search (not auto-highlighted on map).
Low (< 0.40) Unfavorable physical conditions; deep thermocline or warm stratified layer without significant fronts. Available in detail panel via direct coordinate search (not auto-highlighted on map).
Selection limited to North Indian Ocean (5°N–30°N, 45°E–105°E)
